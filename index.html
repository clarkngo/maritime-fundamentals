Course Navigation

A ground-floor introduction for people with no shipping background at all: what ships actually carry, how the industry that owns and runs them is structured, how ports and cargo operations work, what happens on a bridge, and the regulatory and commercial ecosystem — IMO, flag states, classification societies, SOLAS, MARPOL, chartering — that governs all of it. This course builds the industry fluency the companion Maritime OT Security course assumes.

This course carries none of the live-equipment risk of a hands-on OT or engineering course — there is no physical machinery, live vessel, or production network involved anywhere in it. The same care still applies to accuracy, data handling, and responsible use of generated content. These rules apply to every lesson and activity in this course.

Simulation-based scenarios only

Every activity and toggle simulation in this course runs on synthesized, illustrative scenarios. Nothing is built to represent, or to be mistaken for, a specific real vessel's or company's actual operating data.

No real vessel or company data

Case studies (Lesson 11) discuss documented, publicly reported incidents for learning purposes. Coursework, discussion, and any published work should avoid introducing non-public vessel, port, or company data from a learner's own workplace.

Review AI-generated content

Any generated lesson text, quiz item, or graphic gets a subject-matter accuracy review and a source check before it is used or shared further. The instructor owns the accuracy.

Accessible and equitable by default

Materials aim for WCAG 2.2 AA and work by keyboard. No activity depends on a paid license or vendor account a learner cannot obtain.

Case studies are analyzed, not sensationalized

Real incidents (Lesson 11) are examined for cause, impact, and lesson learned — read as case studies to reason about, not retold for dramatic effect or to assign blame beyond what public investigations have established.

No professional certification implied

Completing this course does not confer STCW certification, a professional license, or any formal maritime qualification — it builds industry literacy as a foundation for further study or professional training.

Day 1 · Lesson 3 Foundations
Ports & Terminals

Basic Navigation & Watchkeeping
Bridge team roles, watch schedules, the basic give-way/stand-on logic of COLREGs, voyage planning, and the pilot's role in restricted waters.

Day 2 · Lesson 5 Operations
Cargo Operations
Loading and discharging sequence, stowage planning and basic stability, the IMDG Code and dangerous goods segregation, and what a bill of lading actually does.
Key terms: Stowage Plan, IMDG Code, Bill of Lading, Segregation, Reefer →

A plain-language, non-technical tour of hull structure, watertight subdivision, propulsion, general arrangement, and ballast systems.
Key terms: Keel, Bulkhead, Controllable-Pitch Propeller, General Arrangement, Ballast →
Day 3 · Lesson 7 Regulatory
The Regulatory Ecosystem
Five bodies, five distinct roles: the IMO, flag states, port state control, classification societies, and P&I clubs and insurers.
Key terms: IMO, Flag State, Port State Control, Classification Society, P&I Club →
Day 3 · Lesson 8 Regulatory
Key Maritime Conventions
SOLAS, MARPOL, STCW, the ISM Code, and the ISPS Code — five conventions covering five distinct risk domains, at overview level.
