Content & Scope Boundaries

This course teaches industry, operational, and regulatory literacy about the maritime sector — it carries none of the live-equipment or production-network risk of a hands-on OT, engineering, or security course. That said, accuracy and responsible framing still matter, especially in Lesson 11's incident case studies.

This course builds vocabulary and mental models — it does not confer professional certification, a maritime license, or STCW credentials. Learners and instructors should describe course completion accurately: as a foundational primer, not a professional qualification.

Even though this is not a security course, any discussion of vulnerabilities or gaps (for example, in Lesson 7's discussion of inconsistent flag-state enforcement) stays at the level of understanding the system, not identifying ways to exploit or circumvent a specific real regulator, port, or company's weaknesses.

2

Simulation & Data Practices

Every mapping activity and toggle simulation in this course runs on synthesized, illustrative scenarios.

Nothing in this course's activities is built to represent, or to be mistaken for, a specific real vessel's, port's, or company's actual operating data. Scenario names, figures, and outcomes throughout the twelve lessons are illustrative constructs designed to teach a concept clearly.

Learners who work in or around the maritime industry should draw on synthesized or anonymized detail in any discussion post, written assignment, or capstone project (Lesson 12) rather than an employer's actual configuration, incident history, or commercially sensitive information.

3

Case-Study Boundaries

Lesson 11 examines real, documented maritime incidents — groundings, fires, and pollution events — as case studies in incident causation.

⚠ Case studies are analyzed, not sensationalized

In scope

Analyzing publicly reported incidents for cause, impact, and lessons learned, using the Swiss cheese multi-causal framework from Lesson 11.

Out of scope

Speculative claims beyond what public investigations have established, or framing that assigns blame to a named individual beyond documented findings.

Sourcing

Case-study content should be traceable to public reporting (investigation reports, reputable industry press) rather than presented as unverified rumor.

Sensitivity

Incidents involving loss of life or serious injury are discussed with the same care this course expects throughout: analytical, not sensationalized.

4

Data Handling

The same real-world-data caution from Section 2 extends to any written work a learner produces.

Any real vessel, port, or company detail a learner or instructor encounters outside this course (through employment, research, or personal connections) stays out of shared coursework — discussion posts, published capstone projects, and generated content alike.

Where a real-world example is genuinely useful for teaching, scrub it of identifying detail (vessel name, IMO number, operator, specific routes or figures) or replace it with a synthetic equivalent before using it in a lesson, discussion, or capstone module (Lesson 12).

5

AI-Generated Content Review

Portions of this course's lesson text, quiz items, and supporting material are produced with the help of generative tools. Generated output is treated as a first draft, never as a finished, trusted artifact.

Any AI-generated lesson text, quiz question, or activity description gets a subject-matter accuracy review and a source check before it is used with learners or shared further. Technical claims about vessels, industry structure, and regulation are verified against the sources each lesson and reference page already cites, rather than taken on faith.

Using a generation tool does not transfer responsibility for correctness away from the person who publishes the result. Whoever puts generated material in front of learners is accountable for having reviewed it, the same as any other teaching material they author.

Accessibility & Equity

WCAG 2.2 AA target

Course pages, including this one, aim to meet WCAG 2.2 Level AA — sufficient color contrast, visible focus states, keyboard operability, and semantic structure for assistive technology.

No paid-license dependency

No activity or simulator in this course requires a commercial vendor license, paid software seat, or account a learner cannot freely obtain — everything runs in-browser.

Keyboard operability

Every interactive element — accordions, theme toggle, table of contents, simulations, quizzes — is operable from a keyboard alone.

Reduced-motion support

Pages respect a learner's operating-system preference for reduced motion, disabling non-essential transitions rather than forcing them.

7

Learner Acknowledgment

Because this course carries no live-equipment or production-network risk, no formal sign-off gate is strictly required before starting individual study. For any group teaching activity — most importantly Lesson 12's capstone, where a learner teaches a segment to peers — the following acknowledgment is recommended.

Sample Acknowledgment Statement

"I have read and understood the Safeguards & Responsible Use Policy for Maritime Operations & Industry Fundamentals. I understand that this course's activities are simulation-based, that any real-world examples I introduce into coursework or a capstone module will be scrubbed of identifying detail, and that I will handle case-study material analytically rather than sensationally."

  1. 1

    When it's recommended. Before Lesson 12's capstone teaching activity, where learner-created content will be shared with peers.

  2. 2

    Who collects it. The instructor of record, using whatever mechanism their delivery context supports (a signed form, an LMS quiz gate, or a logged verbal confirmation).

  3. 3

    What it covers. Confirmation that the learner understands the scope boundaries in Sections 1-4 before creating or sharing their own content.

8

Reporting a Concern

Any learner or instructor who believes course content strays outside these boundaries — inaccurate claims presented as fact, real non-public data appearing in shared material, or case-study content that reads as sensationalized rather than analytical — should raise it directly.

  1. 1

    Raise it with the instructor directly. Describe specifically what content or activity seems out of bounds, and which section of this policy it touches.

  2. 2

    Expect the content to be corrected or removed Content flagged as an accuracy or scope-boundary issue should be corrected or removed promptly, not left pending indefinitely.

  3. 3

    Escalate if needed. If the concern involves the instructor's own conduct, or goes unaddressed, escalate through the delivering institution's normal channel.
